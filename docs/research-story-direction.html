03 · the factors

One number that mean-reverts: the crude–gas spread, z-scored.

From price we build the crude–gas spread (a cointegration residual) and standardize it to a 20-day z-score. The economic story is physical: crude and gas are tethered through the energy complex, so the spread wanders but returns.

crude–gas spread · 2024 · oscillates around its mean

z-score · 20 · the tradable signal · ±2 bands

Why these factors? The spread isolates the relationship, not the level (so it's stationary). The z-score makes entries comparable across time. Gas z-score enters as a cross-hedge — it leans against crude-only noise.
04 · does it predict?

The edge is real but shallow — and it decays within the day.

Factor diagnostics. The z-score's information coefficient (rank-corr with the 5-minute forward return) is small but persistently positive — and the marginal IC shows the z-score carries most of it. This is where a quant decides to trust it.

05 · the thesis

Why it should work: physical cointegration, slow to arbitrage.

Crude and gas are linked by substitution and shared macro drivers, but the link is capital-intensive to arbitrage (storage, transport, contract calendars). So the spread overshoots and reverts on a horizon too short for physical players and too capital-heavy for fast ones — a gap a systematic 5-day trade can sit in.

The bet, in one line: when the spread is >2σ from its 20-day mean, fade it — the reversion pays more than the carry costs, except when a real trend takes over (Chapter 07).
06 · the model

Ridge, deliberately boring — interpretable, no overfit.

A ridge regression (α=0.1) on three correlated factors. Why ridge? The factors are collinear (the z-score and the spread move together); ridge keeps the weights stable and readable instead of letting a fancier model overfit a six-month window.

07 · when it works

Mean-reversion pays; trends punish. The drawdown names the risk.

How the regime is computed: a rolling Hurst exponent on the spread (<0.5 = mean-reverting, >0.5 = trending). The strategy is regime-dependent by construction — and every drawdown sits in a trending stretch.

How to read it: the clay band in March is the one trending stretch — and it's exactly the drawdown in Chapter 01. The next move (slower z-score) trades fewer of these false reversions.
