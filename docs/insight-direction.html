the finding

Mean-reversion on the crude–gas spread held its edge out-of-sample.

A 20-day z-score on the WTI–Henry-Hub spread, traded with a 5-day hold over 2024. 1.4× return per unit of risk, ~19% a year — and it kept working on data it never saw while fitting.

what drove it · ridge model

The 20-day z-score carries the signal — gas leans against it.

The model's weights, learned on 2024: the crude z-score does the work, the gas z-score pulls the other way (a hedge), and the raw spread barely matters. ridge · α=0.1

when it worked

It paid in mean-reverting weeks — and gave it back in one March trend.

Regime over the eval window. The edge is real when the spread oscillates (mean-reverting); the only drawdown lines up exactly with a five-week trending-down stretch in March — the honest risk, named.
