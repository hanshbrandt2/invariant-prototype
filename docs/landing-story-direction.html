01 · the finding

Up 9.4% over 26 weeks — but every dollar was earned in the chop, and part of it handed back in the trend.

Weekly cumulative return. It climbed to +8.0% by late February, then the trending-DOWN weeks dragged it to −0.3% by late March — a −8.3% drawdown — before reverting to +9.4%. The regime ribbon under the curve is the tell: the clay weeks are where it fell.

02 · the factors

Three signals went into the matrix — the z-score and the spread move together; gas runs on its own.

zscore_20 (crude log-return z-score, 20-day), gas_z20 (nat-gas z-score, same window), and spread_5d (the crude–gas cointegrated spread, 60-bar lookback). They aren’t redundant: zscore_20 and spread_5d correlate 0.58 — they revert together — while gas_z20 is nearly independent (0.31, −0.12), which is exactly why it can hedge in the next chapter.

03 · what drove it

“Z-score carries the signal — gas z-score leans against it.”

A ridge baseline (α=0.1) on the signal matrix, predicting the 5-minute forward return, learned three weights: zscore_20 +0.42 carries it, spread_5d +0.09 adds a little in the same direction, and gas_z20 −0.18 leans against it — damping crude-only noise.

04 · when it works

It paid in mean-reverting weeks — and gave it back when the market trended.

The edge is regime-dependent by construction. Across 26 weeks, mean-reversion ruled 17, the market trended up for 2 and down for 5, and 2 were no-trade. The five trending-DOWN weeks (Feb 27 – Mar 26) overlap the drawdown exactly — that’s where equity slid from +8.0% to −0.3%.

05 · how it was built

Seven steps from 3.8M raw crude bars to a validated backtest — every node hashed.

crude_oil_1m (3.8M rows) and nat_gas_1m (Henry Hub) stitch to a continuous front-month under the Panama calendar-roll policy — no artificial roll gap — then log-returns, three signals, the matrix, a 5-minute-ahead target, the ridge model, and a dollar-neutral top-decile long/short backtest. The z-window was widened 14→20 after a turnover review.
